Concept 1

Opinion vs. Developed Argument

Definition: An opinion simply states a preference. A developed argument states a claim, explains the reasoning behind it, and often predicts a consequence — it can survive the question "why?" being asked twice.
Weak — opinion only
Free education is good because it helps students.
Developed argument
Providing free university education can improve social mobility because it allows talented students from low-income families to pursue degrees without accumulating debt. For instance, students who could not otherwise afford tuition might gain access to professions such as medicine or engineering.
Second developed example
Investing in public transport can reduce urban pollution because fewer people rely on private cars for daily commuting. As a result, cities that expand their metro networks often see measurable drops in air-quality complaints within a few years.

Concept 2

The Argument Detective

Definition: Examiners reward paragraphs that are specific and supported. A weak paragraph repeats the same idea in different words; a strong one explains a mechanism and gives a concrete example.
Paragraph A
Nowadays, technology is very important in education. Students use computers because they are useful. Technology is good for everyone, and schools should use it more.
Paragraph B
Online education provides students with greater flexibility. They can study whenever they want and do not need to travel to university. This is particularly beneficial for people with jobs or family responsibilities.
Paragraph C
Online education can widen access to higher education, particularly for students in remote areas. Since digital courses eliminate the need to commute, learners can pursue degrees without incurring accommodation costs. For example, a student in a rural area could attend lectures from a university in the capital without leaving home.

Concept 3

The PEEL Formula

Definition: A four-part structure for building any body paragraph: state a Point, Explain why it's true, give an Example, then Link back to the question.
P
Point — One disadvantage of social media is its potential to undermine young people's self-esteem.
E
Explanation — Adolescents are frequently exposed to curated images of others' lifestyles, creating unrealistic expectations.
E
Example — Teenagers who compare themselves to influencers may feel dissatisfied, even though the images are digitally enhanced.
L
Link — As a result, excessive exposure to idealised content can contribute to emotional distress.

Concept 4

Paragraph Surgery

Definition: Revision means fixing three common illnesses: repetition (saying the same thing twice), vague vocabulary (words like "good," "bad," "important"), and an underdeveloped claim (no explanation of why it's true).
Patient
Many people think children should not use smartphones. Smartphones are bad because children use them too much. They can also affect their health. This is why smartphones are harmful.
After surgery
Excessive smartphone use can negatively affect children's physical and cognitive development. Prolonged screen time may disrupt sleep, particularly when devices are used late at night. For instance, a child who plays mobile games until midnight may struggle to concentrate the next morning.
Game

Upgrade the Vocabulary

Match each vague word to its more precise replacement.

Concept 5

Writing a Real Conclusion

Definition: A conclusion restates the position in new words, summarises the main arguments, and may add a final implication — but never introduces a brand-new argument.
Introduction (for reference)
Some people believe that governments should make university education free for all citizens. I largely agree because free education can promote equal opportunities and strengthen the economy.
Sample conclusion
In conclusion, making university education accessible without tuition fees can promote social equality while also contributing to long-term economic development. By removing financial obstacles, governments can create opportunities for individuals and strengthen the workforce.

Concept 6

The 8 Components of a Well-Developed Argument

Definition: A well-developed argument goes beyond stating an opinion and giving an example. It shows a clear line of reasoning, explains causes or consequences, and builds a logical connection between ideas.
Complete example — body paragraph. Some people believe private cars should be banned from city centres. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

T → E → EL → EX → A → C

Topic · Explanation · Elaboration · Example · Analysis · Consequence. Add Qualification and a Concluding sentence when they serve a purpose — never force all eight.

Most important teaching rule: Explanation, example and analysis are different jobs. Every example must answer: "So what does this demonstrate?"
